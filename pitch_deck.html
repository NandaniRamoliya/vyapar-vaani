Build for Bharat · Livelihoods and small business · Round 2

VyaparVaani

The first khata where the customer’s own voice is the signature.

Vaani is a voice munim for Indian shops. The shopkeeper speaks in English, Hindi or Gujarati. Vaani writes the udhar, asks the customer to confirm it in their own voice, and seals that recording so it can be played back in any dispute. It works on the phone the shop already has, with zero API keys.

1 · Problem

Udhar runs on trust and a notebook, and it breaks at the moment of a dispute.

“Maine kab liya?” · “Itna nahi tha!” · “Aapne galat likha hoga.”

Small shops (kirana, cloth, hardware, wholesale traders) give goods on credit every day. The record is a handwritten khata or, at best, an app entry that only the shopkeeper typed. When a customer disputes it, it is the shopkeeper’s word against theirs.

Why today’s tools don’t fix it

  • Hands are busy cutting cloth, weighing and counting cash, so typing at the counter doesn’t happen.
  • Many customers can’t read or sign, so a written or typed entry is not proof they agreed.
  • Apps are English-first forms. Shopkeepers think and talk in Hindi, Gujarati or Hinglish.
  • Digital ledgers digitised the notebook, not the trust. Money is lost, follow-ups are awkward, relationships suffer.

1 · Solution

Vaani: speak the khata, and let the customer confirm it in their own voice.

1 · SHOPKEEPER SPEAKS

“Rahul ko udhar likho.”

Vaani asks what is missing: “How many rupees?” → “5000”.

2 · VAANI CONFIRMS

“₹5,000 udhar. Balance becomes ₹20,000. Shall I write it?”

Shopkeeper says “Haan ji” (no tap).

3 · CUSTOMER SIGNS BY VOICE

“Haan, maine 5000 udhar liye.”

Recorded on the phone, stops by itself when they finish.

4 · SEALED

SHA-256 fingerprint of the audio, hash-chained to every earlier signature. Edits or deletions are detected.

5 · DISPUTE? PLAY IT

“Rahul ka saboot sunao.”

Rahul’s own voice plays. Share the clip on WhatsApp in one tap.

Talks like a person

Asks follow-up questions, remembers “uska / उसका / એનું”, understands “Rahul ne diye” (he paid) vs “Rahul ko diye” (you gave), lets you interrupt, undoes mistakes by voice, gives the day’s hisaab and who to chase tomorrow.

Bharat languages, even mixed

English, हिन्दी, ગુજરાતી, Hinglish, or all three in one sentence. Vaani detects the language you spoke (even English written as “आई वांट टू…” by a Hindi recogniser), replies in it and switches the app. Names in any script match the book (राहुल / રાહુલે → Rahul Sharma).

Phone-first platform, zero keys

Thumb-reach mic, installs as an app, reminders that ring and notify. One server hosts many shops: each has its own login and its own database, and an admin console adds and manages shops. No paid AI API, no secret keys, one Docker command.

Working today: 143 automated tests · multi-shop logins with isolated data · tested end to end on an emulated Android phone and in Docker3

2 · Who it is for

Shopkeepers who sell on credit, and the customers who buy from them.

6.3 crore

MSMEs in India (Ministry of MSME, NSS 73rd round: 633.88 lakh enterprises). Most are micro businesses run by one family.

1 crore+

Kirana and neighbourhood stores (industry estimates), the most common place where daily udhar happens.

58 crore+

People speak Hindi or Gujarati (Census 2011: Hindi 52.8 crore, Gujarati 5.5 crore). These are the languages Vaani works in today.

Primary user: the shop owner

  • 40–55 years old, fluent in Gujarati or Hindi, uses WhatsApp daily and avoids typing.
  • Gives udhar to 20–200 regular customers. Loses money to forgotten or disputed entries.
  • Wants: “Just tell me who owes me, and give me proof when they argue.”

Second user: the customer

  • Confirms with their voice. No app, no signature, and literacy is not needed.
  • Gets reminders and receipts on WhatsApp in their own language.
  • Often a shopkeeper themselves (wholesale chains), so every customer is a potential next user.
Beachhead: Gujarat textile and kirana markets (Surat, Ahmedabad, Rajkot), then Hindi belt4

4 · Launch plan

Start in one market, win shop by shop, let every reminder recruit the next shop.

First 50 shops (month 1–2)

  • Pick one dense market (e.g., a Surat textile market) and onboard shops in person, in Gujarati.
  • Install in 2 minutes: open a link, then “Add to Home screen”. No app store, no sign-up form.
  • Import the top 20 udhar customers by voice during onboarding.

Grow through the customers (month 3–6)

  • Every WhatsApp reminder, receipt and voice proof carries “Sent with Vaani”.
  • Wholesale customers are shopkeepers too, so each signed udhar shows Vaani to a new shop.
  • Partner with vyapari mandals (trader associations) and distributors’ salesmen who visit shops weekly.

Scale and earn (month 6+)

  • Core khata stays free. Paid plan: backups, multiple staff phones, scheduled reminders.
  • Partnerships with UPI soundbox and payment players, so payments land directly in the khata.
  • With the shop’s consent, the voice-verified credit history can support working-capital lending.

What we measure

Weekly active shops · entries made by voice · share of udhar that is voice-signed · money collected after reminders · disputes resolved with a played proof.

For the finale

On-device speech for weak networks, more languages (Marathi, Tamil), scheduled reminders with UPI pay links, multi-device sync, and a pilot with real shops.
